### Core Rules

The core mechanic of _BareBones D12 Edition_ revolves around rolling a d12 and adding the relevant attribute modifier. The result is then compared to the **difficulty rating** of the test for social encounter or combat action.

- **A natural 12** on the d12 is an **automatic success**, even without adding any modifiers. Conversely, **a natural 1** is a **fumble**.
- When a fumble occurs, the referee inflicts a **hard move** on the player character.

Any type of roll done during game is called a test. Every test is tied to an attribute. 

The referee sets a **Difficulty Rating (DR)** that the player must **meet or exceed with their roll**.

### Character Creation

_BareBones RPG_ encourages open character concepts. Players can begin fleshing out their characters with the referee during the stat rolling process.

The referee will have a back-and-forth conversation with the players and may allow them to adjust their attribute points and assign extra bonuses based on their chosen **background**. Backgrounds will be covered in more detail later in this document.

#### Down The Line (DTL with Colored d4s)

1. You'll use two different colored 4-sided dice (d4), one representing a positive modifier and the other negative.
2. Roll both dice "Down the Line" (DTL) for each attribute (Might, Agility, Grit, Insight). Add the results of the two dice together to get your modifier for that attribute (range of -3 to +3).

#### Archetype Requisite (d6-d4)
1. If you choose an archetype (Fighter, Cleric, Magic-User, Thief), there may be a specific ability score requirement for it.
2. Instead of using the colored d4s for this specific attribute, roll a 6-sided die (d6) and subtract the result of another 4-sided die (d4) to determine your modifier.

If player gets all negative, referee will allow player to do a single re-reroll again and get a a mix of both positive and negative.

#### Attributes

- **Might:** *Crush, lift, strike, grapple*
- **Agility:** *Defend, balance, swim, flee*
- **Grit:** *Resist poison, cold, heat, survive falling*
- **Will**: *Resolve, spell casting, ritual, and resistance*
- **Insight:** *Perceive, aim, charm, and smarts*

#### Archetypes

- **Fighter** (*Brawler, close combat, melee weapon expert, engages enemies in the front line*)

	- Fighters rely on their **Might** for combat and other tests related to this attribute.

- **Cleric** (*Holy or divine power, healer, supports companions*)

	- Clerics rely on their **Insight** for praying, healing, and other tests related to this attribute.
  
- **Magic-User** (*Studious and knowledgeable of the occult, Magic-Users stay in the back rank, wielding powerful spells.*)

	- Magic-Users rely on their **Will** for remembering spells, casting them effectively, and passing other tests related to their magical aptitude.
	
- **Thief** (*Schemes for the right moment to strike, hidden in the shadows, and knows a thing or two about traps!*)
	- Thieves rely on their **Agility** for sneaking, avoiding detection, and staying on their feet – all skills related to this attribute.
	
> **Optional Rule:**
> If player wants to add more points to their required attribute, they can subtract up to 2 points from another attribute. 

### Class

When making a character, players can still roll **1d4-1d4** DTL, however, they need to adjust their stat with the class' ability given. 

#### Cleric

- **War Priest:** (+1 Might, +1 Will)  Capable of wielding weapons and armor in addition to healing and divine magic.

- **Inquisitor:** (+2 Insight, -1 Agility)  Expert at detecting and rooting out evil, with a focus on offensive magic.

- **Druid:** (+2 Will, -1 Insight)  Draws power from nature, specializing in healing and control magic related to plants and animals.

#### Fighter

- **Berserker:**  (+2 Might, -1 Grit)  Relishes close combat and deals additional damage when wounded.

- **Knight:** (+1 Might, +1 Grit)  Proficient with a variety of weapons and armor, excels in mounted combat.

- **Duelist:** (+2 Agility, -1 Might)  Focuses on finesse and speed in combat, specializing in parrying and counter-attacking.

#### Magic-User

- **Wizard:** (+2 Will, -1 Insight)  Master of a wide array of spells, focusing on elemental and arcane magic.

- **Warlock:** (+1 Will, +1 Insight)  Draws power from otherworldly patrons, specializing in curses and manipulation magic.

- **Enchanter:** (+2 Insight, -1 Will)  Expert at creating magical items and enchantments, with a focus on illusions and charm magic.

#### Thief

- **Assassin:** (+2 Agility, -1 Grit)  Trained in stealth and deadly combat, specializing in poisons and critical strikes.

- **Bard:** (+1 Agility, +1 Insight)  Uses music and storytelling to inspire allies and manipulate enemies, with some proficiency in thievery and social interactions.

- **Ranger:** (+2 Grit, -1 Agility)  Adept at wilderness survival and tracking, proficient with ranged weapons and light armor.


### Calculating Hit Points

In _BareBones_, a character's starting maximum **Hit Points** are determined by their **GRIT** score plus a d10 roll. Or referee can allow 13 plus **GRIT** for a stronger hero.

### How Much Can I Hold?

A character's carrying capacity is determined by their Might score. You can carry a total of **8 + your Might** modifier in slots.

#### Encumbered

If you carry more than your capacity, you become encumbered. Here's what happens when encumbered:

- **Movement**: Your movement speed is halved.
- **Defense**: You suffer a **penalty** to your **Defense** rolls. The referee can increase the DR depending on the situation. Making it harder for the character to avoid the incoming hit.

### Generating Currency

1. The referee will let you know what type of currency is used in the game world (e.g., gold pieces in a fantasy setting, dollars in a modern setting). This currency can be used to purchase your starting gear, weapon, and armor.

2. The player will roll 3d6 * 10 (fantasy setting) or 3d6 * 100 (modern setting) for money, gold, or credit. This currency can be used to purchase their starting gear, weapon, and armor.

## Backgrounds

Players are encouraged to create rich backgrounds for their characters. This backstory fleshes out your character's personality, motivations, and past experiences. When a situation arises in the game that relates to your character's background, you can explain it to the referee. If your explanation is convincing and highlights a relevant skill or knowledge, the referee can award you a bonus for your related skill check.

The referee has the discretion to acknowledge your character's background in a few ways:

- **Circumstantial Advantage**: The referee might describe a circumstantial benefit your character gains due to their background (e.g., spotting a hidden passage because you were trained as a dungeon delver).
- **Informational Hint**: The referee might offer a helpful hint or piece of information related to your character's background (e.g., recalling a legend about a weakness of the creature you're facing). 

Remember, a convincing background explanation increases the chances of receiving a bonus, but it's ultimately the referee's call. By effectively using backgrounds, you can add depth and meaning to your character's actions and contribute to a more immersive story in the game world.

|      Easy       |  6   |

|      Hard       |  10  |
|    Very Hard    |  12  |


### Battle Difficulty Rating

The referee sets a **Difficulty Rating (DR)** for the attack based on the attacker's weapon, enemy's size, and the situation. Here are some examples: 
While Hit Dice (dx) is a good indicator of a creature's resilience,  battle difficulty can also be influenced by other factors.

|  Size  |  DR  |
| :----: | :--: |
|  Tiny  |  4   |
| Small  |  6   |
| Medium |  8   |
| Large  |  10  |
| Huge+  |  12  |

While Hit Dice (dx) is a good indicator of a creature's resilience,  battle difficulty can also be influenced by other factors like armor, conditions (poison, blindness, or other status), terrain, and visibility

Enemies with armor and shield will increase the DR to hit same as players. 

> **For Example:** 

> A Dwarf cleric NPC will have a DR (8+2+1=11) due to wearing a chainmail armor and holding a shield. 

> A Goblin wearing light armor will have a DR (6+1=7)

## Action and Movement

Player's turn: they can move up to 30 feet, attack, defend, or wait for a reaction. If the player uses double movement, they can move up to 60 feet. Then, the player will wait for the next round to attack.

- Whenever an *attack is declared*, the player will use **MIGHT** when a weapon has a close-range tag.

- Whenever an *attack is declared*, the player will use **INSIGHT** when a weapon has a mid or far range due to aiming.

- Whenever a character *needs to defend*, the player will use **AGILIT**Y when wearing armor, including the worn armor tag.

- Whenever a player *casts a spell*, they will use **WILL**.

- Whenever a player is bitten by something or touches something poisonous, the PC will use **GRIT** to save.

## Initiative Rules

In a battle encounter, a designated player rolls a die on behalf of their group to determine the starting order. The player turns to initiate from the left side of the GM and progresses clockwise around the table.

- 1-6... **Enemies** go first
- 7-12... **Players** go first

### Combat Action

#### Attacking

During their turn, a player can choose to attack an enemy within their reach. The success of the attack depends on several factors:

- **Weapon Choice**: The type of weapon a character wields can affect the attack. For instance, a sword might be better for close-quarters combat (using **Might**), while a bow might be better for ranged attacks (using **Insight** for aiming).
- **Target Difficulty**: The referee (GM) will set a **Difficulty Rating (DR)** for the attack based on the difficulty of hitting the target (e.g., DR 8 for a *stationary enemy* vs. DR 12 for a *moving enemy*)Also, armor can affect DR, the heavier the armor is character may take penalty attacking.
- **Attribute Roll**: The player will roll a d12 and add their relevant attribute modifier based on their weapon choice (Might for melee, Insight for ranged aiming).

#### Defense

The player rolls a d12 and adds their **Agility modifier** to the roll.

### Enemy's Turn

The referee will ask the players in combat for an **Agility test**. **If a player meets or beats the enemy's DR (Defense Rating), they avoid the attack.** If the **player fails the test**, the enemy will **attack and deal damage** to the character. 

### Critical Fail

This is the only time the enemy will do **double damage and reduce the character's armor tier** by one. This will only happen when a player rolls a critical failure or a fumble on their **Defense test**.

### Referee Doesn't Roll for Enemies

Players roll **Defense** (*Agility*) during combat. If their roll **beats** the enemy's **DR (*Defense Rating*)**, they avoid the attack.

If the player is attacking, they then roll with their weapon's appropriate attribute (Might, Insight, etc.). If their roll **meets or surpasses** the DR, they hit the enemy and proceed to **roll damage dice**.

> **MORALE:**
>
> The referee can use the DR as a gauge of enemy morale. When the enemy leader is defeated **or** the enemy loses half of its hit points, the player can roll 2d6 for a morale check.

#### Armor:

Different types of armor provide the player's different **armor reduction**. Due to armor being heavy it may increase **DR** when attacking. The PCs will have an attack penalty when trying to hit a creature.

### Critical Attack & Defense

#### Critical Attack
If a player rolls a natural 12, this is a **critical hit**. The player will do **double the damage** against the enemy. This attack will reduce the enemy's armor. The enemies' armor will reduce depending on what is wearing. 

>  This will decrease from a *d8 >> d6 >> d4 >> d2 >> 0*.  This will temporary decreases the armor reduction until it is repaired.  

#### Critical Defense
If player rolls a 12, this is a **Critical dodge**. The player will see an opening and get a free attack. Player will roll damage. **No hit-roll is required**. Roll weapon damage die.

### Bones Luck 

Players will begin with 4 **Bones Luck**. Bones Luck can help change roll results:

- DR -4 **Combat Roll**
- DR -4 **Mobility Save** 
- **+1 Initiative Roll** to change the outcome of the initiative result. Perfect for scenario player rolled a 6 and they can add the 1 to change it to 7 and players can go first

For example: 
Player character rolled a 6 in combat and is unable to beat creature's DR. **PC can burn** a Bones luck to make their roll successful. 

> The Fighter, wielding a melee weapon, attacks with their MIGHT attribute. They roll a 3 on the d12, and add their MIGHT of 3, resulting in a total of 6.  The Orc Raider has a Difficulty Rating (DR) of 9 (8 base + 1 from armor). Normally, the Fighter would need to roll a 9 or higher to hit. However, they choose to use their **Bones Luck (BL)**, which reduces the target's DR by 4. This lowers the Orc Raider's DR to 5. Since the Fighter's roll of 6 now meets or exceeds the reduced DR, the attack is successful. The Fighter may roll damage.

Players can regain their **Bones Luck** after a long rest. 



### Burn

Player can push a test after a failed roll. The player can roll their test again and they will start burning their modifier points.  

> Example:

> **Player:** "I rolled a 3 on my combat test."

> **Referee:** "A 3 will not meet the DR on the test. You are out of luck! However, you can Burn your Might!  You can re-roll your test and take the best result.:

> **Player:** "I think I will do that! "

> **Referee:** "Once you do this roll, your Might will go down by a point.  Until you have a few days of rest."

> **Player:** "I will take that!" (Rolls a d12 and rolls a 10) "Yes!!!"

Players can recover their points depending on how much burn they get in combat or social encounter:

| Burn | Time to Recover|
| :--: | :--------------------- |
|  1   | Two days            |
|  2   | 4 days                 |
|  3   | A week                |
|  4   | 2 weeks and two days   |
|  5   | 3 weeks and three days |
